# Amigo Studios: shared valuation and handover notes

Prepared November 2026 for Austin Faure (75%) and Pat Lang (25%). **Confidential:** this includes lease terms covered by the lease's confidentiality clause (§9), which allows them to be shared with prospective assignees. Please keep it between the owners, any incoming partners, and your advisors.

This is a working fact base for the two of us. It isn't an appraisal, and it isn't legal or tax advice. Every number traces back to the Mercury records (Mar 4 – Nov 10, 2026), the lease, or the equipment inventory. Where something is an estimate, it's labeled as one.

## 1. Where the studio stands

**Running costs (after Jul 17, per month):** rent + CAM + insurance $3,818 · utilities ~$1,000 · studio hosting (Jacob) ~$360 · supplies, software, platform fees, ads and other ~$580 · podcast-services crew ~$1,040 · ~$250 estimated for costs outside Mercury (insurance, tax prep, California $800 LLC tax). **What changed:** spring was strong, with Austin working about 20 hours a week. Most podcast-services work comes from two clients.

**Early November:** Nov 1–10 brought in **$5,161** of studio revenue (Stripe $3,523, Peerspace $1,040, subtenant $598). That's a strong start. A full November, plus prior-year Stripe and Peerspace history, would show whether October was a low point or the new normal.

## 2. What's being transferred

Furniture is valued from what was actually paid (two of the couches were free). Pat's tube lights are excluded. The strobes, hazer, floppies and green screens belong to the cyclorama studio rental and are included. The makeup station, pony wall and closet shelving are counted in the build-out below, not here.

**Build-out** (stays with the space): the cyc wall, front-room dividers, the upstairs podcast-studio wall, soundproofing, the custom downstairs office, plus the pony wall, makeup station, closet shelving, slatted divider and shelves, wall panels, kitchenette and pendants. It cost **about $60k**. It's attached to the building, and under a standard lease it belongs to the landlord when the lease ends, so it has no resale value. What it's worth is the **right to keep using it**, which lasts only as long as a lease. Walls and soundproofing last far longer than a lease (assume ~15 years, so about $4k a year of use). That's ~$2.2k for the 6.5 months left on the current term. After May 2027 the space and its build-out are the landlord's, and the renewal is what lets the studio keep using them. The value section splits the renewal-term use evenly between seller and buyer: **~$7k for a ~2–3-year renewal, ~$10k for ~4 years, ~$12k for 5 years**.

**Website and booking system:** a custom site and booking system: card-hold booking with team approval, automatic texts and emails (reminders, abandoned-booking recovery, review requests), an admin and crew hub, design-your-set, and ~20 local SEO pages. Rebuilding it would cost roughly $45–150k. Pat and/or his team get full access and use; Austin and the buyers each keep a non-exclusive right to resell or license the booking system to other studios. The site's SEO pages and the studio's own setup are fully the buyers'.

## 3. The lease, in plain terms

- **Landlord:** Live Wire Investments LLC. **Premises:** 825 South Coast Highway, Oceanside. About **1,750 sq ft**. Gross lease.
- **Rent:** $3,691/mo base + ~$65 CAM + ~$62 insurance = **$3,818/mo all-in** (≈ $2.18/sq ft). That's roughly in line with asking rents on the S Coast Hwy corridor.
- **Ends May 31, 2027.** There's no renewal option or rate in the lease, so a renewal has to be negotiated. Past increases have been about 4% a year. A 4% renewal would add about $150/mo; a market-rate renewal (~$2.40/sq ft) about $380/mo.
- **Who's on it:** three individuals, **Jeremiah Idema, Austin Faure and Beau Trembly**, jointly. Jeremiah and Beau are past partners who moved out of state around late 2024 and have no stake in the current business. **The current LLC isn't named on the lease.**
- **What that means:** the space can't move with the LLC by itself. The handover needs a **landlord-approved new lease (or assignment and amendment) to the buyers' entity (Pat and/or his team), combined with the renewal past May 2027, that releases Austin, Jeremiah and Beau.** The 2024 First Amendment did exactly this when it released Brandon Gleed, so there's a template. The landlord will likely ask for the new tenant's financials and possibly a guarantee.

## 4. Value

**Expected price for Austin's 75%: ~$32k**

The whole studio is worth ~$43k. Pat already owns 25%, so Pat and/or his new partners buy the other 75% (75% × $43k ≈ $32k). Depending on lease length and bookings, the price for the 75% could land between ~$22k and ~$55k.

**How the ~$43k studio value adds up** (renewal of ~3–5 years, studio kept running):

| Piece | Value | Basis |
|---|---:|---|
| Gear and furniture, in use | $16.5k | Movable equipment and furniture, valued as set up in a working studio (Pat's rig excluded) |
| Build-out use | $10.2k | ~$2.2k to May 2027 + half of ~4 years × ~$4k a year of use on the ~$60k build-out |
| Listings & reputation | $4.0k | The Peerspace listings' 95 five-star reviews. A brand-new listing would start with none and likely get fewer bookings for its first year or two |
| Website and booking system | $12.0k | $15k credit for the site, booking system and SEO pages, reduced because the booking system is non-exclusive |
| **Whole studio** | **$42.7k ≈ $43k** | |
| **Austin's 75% (× 75%) = the price** | **$32.0k ≈ $32k** | |

**Checked against earnings:** since July 17 the studio makes about $26.7k a year before anyone is paid, nearly all of it from podcast services for two clients. Counting that podcast profit at 60% (it depends on those clients staying) gives ~$15k a year; at a typical 2× multiple that's ~$30k, plus the website ($12k) as a separate asset: **~$42k**, close to the ~$43k above. The price uses the higher of the two, never both added together.

**What moves it within the range:**
- **Shorter renewal, low-end credits → ~$22k for the 75%** (whole studio ~$29k): gear at used value ($12.2k), build-out use $7.2k (2–3 years), listings $3k, website $6.5k.
- **Both podcast-services clients stay → ~$55k for the 75%** (whole studio ~$74k): the full ~$26.7k a year at 2× ($53k) plus the website at the top of its range ($20.5k). An earn-out on podcast-services revenue fits this case best.

**Cost to recreate: ~$155–285k**, including the ~$60k build-out and the $45–150k website and booking system. That's what the buyers avoid by not building from scratch; it's a setup cost, not a price.

No minority discount: the 75% sells as one controlling block. Cash and liabilities (card balances, prepaid bookings, taxes) settle separately at closing.

## 5. Deal-structure options

- **Purchase of the 75% interest, or an asset purchase by the buyers' entity** (Pat and/or his team). Either works. Since the LLC isn't on the lease, an asset purchase into that entity plus a new lease directly with the landlord may be the simpler route. Worth a quick check with a lawyer and CPA.
- **Cash at closing** (simplest), or a **seller note:** part at closing, the rest over 12–18 months with interest, secured by the equipment, once the new lease is signed.
- **Earn-out:** a capped share of revenue above the after-July-17 baseline (~$9.4k/mo, rooms plus podcast services) for 12 months, measured from Stripe, Peerspace and the bank.
- **Referral fee** for new work Austin sends later, separate from the price.
