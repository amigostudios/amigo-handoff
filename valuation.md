# Amigo Studios: shared valuation and handover notes

Prepared November 2026 for Austin Faure (75%) and Pat Lang (25%). **Confidential:** this includes lease terms covered by the lease's confidentiality clause (§9), which allows them to be shared with prospective assignees. Please keep it between the owners, any incoming partners, and your advisors.

This is a working fact base for the two of us. It isn't an appraisal, and it isn't legal or tax advice. Every number traces back to the Mercury records (Mar 4 – Nov 10, 2026), the lease, or the equipment inventory. Where something is an estimate, it's labeled as one.

## 3. The lease, in plain terms

- **Landlord:** Live Wire Investments LLC. **Premises:** 825 South Coast Highway, Oceanside. About **1,750 sq ft**. Gross lease.
- **Rent:** $3,691/mo base + ~$65 CAM + ~$62 insurance = **$3,818/mo all-in** (≈ $2.18/sq ft). That's roughly in line with asking rents on the S Coast Hwy corridor.
- **Ends May 31, 2027.** There's no renewal option or rate in the lease, so a renewal has to be negotiated. Past increases have been about 4% a year. A 4% renewal would add about $150/mo; a market-rate renewal (~$2.40/sq ft) about $380/mo.
- **Who's on it:** three individuals, **Jeremiah Idema, Austin Faure and Beau Trembly**, jointly. Jeremiah and Beau are past partners who moved out of state around late 2024 and have no stake in the current business. **The current LLC isn't named on the lease.**
- **What that means:** the space can't move with the LLC by itself. The handover needs a **landlord-approved new lease (or assignment and amendment) to Pat's entity, combined with the renewal past May 2027, that releases Austin, Jeremiah and Beau.** The 2024 First Amendment did exactly this when it released Brandon Gleed, so there's a template. The landlord will likely ask for the new tenant's financials and possibly a guarantee or deposit.
- **Security deposit: $3,691**, held by the landlord ($2,971 paid in 2021 + $720 in 2024). It most likely came from the original lessees, so it's handled **outside the business value**: the new tenant reimburses whoever funded it, and the deposit moves to the new lease (or the landlord refunds it and takes a new one). Who funded it, and in what shares, still needs to be confirmed.

## 5. Deal-structure options

- **Purchase of the 75% interest, or an asset purchase by Pat's entity.** Either works. Since the LLC isn't on the lease, an asset purchase into Pat's entity plus a new lease directly with the landlord may be the simpler route. Worth a quick check with a lawyer and CPA.
- **Cash at closing.** The simplest option.
- **Seller note.** Part paid at closing, the rest over 12–18 months with interest, secured by the equipment. Typically starts once the new lease is signed.
- **Earn-out tied to studio revenue.** For example, a share of studio revenue above the Jun–Oct baseline (~$5.7k/mo) for 12 months, capped. It's easy to measure from Stripe, Peerspace and the bank. Monthly reporting keeps it simple.
- **Referral fee.** If Austin keeps referring work to the studio, a simple agreement such as a percentage of fees on referred jobs, kept separate from the purchase price.
